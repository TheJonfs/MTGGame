## Deviations from the brief
1. **Fifteen seeds drafted, thirteen kept.** The brief asked twelve to fourteen; the mill and the WB control were its own ideas and measured under 15.
2. **Part 5 has no rule.** The probe showed the candidate rule's premise (nobody attacks for N turns) describes one game in fifty-one. What was measured instead is the turn limit.
3. **Part 3's noise run is random swaps, not rating noise.** S51 built noise decks with the rating perturbed; a format with no rating of its own has nothing to perturb, so each seed has eight nonland cards replaced at random within its colours.
4. **The Open's thresholds for `small` and `large` are kept as numbers**, and only the other formats' are computed from ranks — computing the Open's from its field would have moved them (15 → 17) and with them every measured guide.
5. **Seed revisions were not measured card by card** (ADR-168's bar). The planner's revisions were applied as written; the one found wrong (Sign in Blood) turned out to be the pilot. The Longbow Archers, the Captains and the Marshals are in their lists unmeasured against what they replaced.
6. **The four-day Convocation with a Peasant day was not played through in the browser** — the door's choosers were, and a short Convocation with a Peasant day runs headless to a champion in the test.

## Concerns
1. **The turn limit is what draws games, and it is a ruling.** A hundred turns is short of a library by about seven. At 140 the Open's 128 turn-limit draws and Peasant's 45 would all be results — mostly losses for the Tollgate, which runs out of cards first. Raising `maxTurns` (120 is enough) changes the Tollgate's number and nothing else measurable; it is the engine's default and the planner's call.
2. **Dungrove Auras is over ADR-171's line on a first draft (70.5; 65 against a spread in game one).** Nothing in Peasant targets a hexproof creature except an Edict (Diabolic Edict is tier two and in no list), a sweeper (the Wave kills it — its power is its toughness) or a blocker. The lists were not built knowing it existed. Whether it is the format's pillar or its problem is the next table's question, with sideboards that know about it.
3. **The planner's control ideas did not survive.** UB Mill 15, WB Wave Control 13, UR Pyroclasm 28. Wave of Reckoning is cast a third of a game where it is played and its decks are 42–43. Peasant on this evidence is midrange-and-aggro with one hexproof deck on top; the gaps list in `docs/penny-experiments.md` (a sweeper that matters, card advantage) stands — Harmonize went to the best deck, not to control.
4. **WB Wave Control's 13 may be the pilot.** It is the first list whose top end cannot be cast and must be buried and returned; nothing teaches the pilot that in a list without a plan (plans are the Open's combo lists'). Not separated.
5. **A book found by a seed list is a reminder**: Sign in Blood was miscast for as long as it has been in the pool's vocabulary ("target player draws"), and no list held one. Book 123's lesson again — the cast count showed nothing (it was cast 1.2 times a game, at the wrong head).
6. **The Peasant score rates the cards of the decks that exist.** Dungrove Elder is first because its deck wins; a card no seed plays is rated from 78 noise decks. It is better than the Limited score for this shelf and should be rebuilt when the seeds change.
7. **`card-rating.json` gained rows for the ten new cards** (their tier prior as `rating`) so that they could carry a Peasant score. The Limited score has no number for them: in Sealed and draft they read their tier's prior until a Limited run includes them.
8. **Guides and out-rules are matched by cards across all formats.** A Peasant list with the same fifteen as an Open list would take its guide. None does.
