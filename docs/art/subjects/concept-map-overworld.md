# Concept: the overworld map, upgraded (S20 playtest round 1)

Direction-setting concept plate, not a shipping asset. Chris asked (playtest
round 1, item 3) what an appearance upgrade to the world map could look like —
this explores the "antique campaign map" register the current SVG map could
grow toward: hand-hatched terrain in place of flat washes, inked region
borders, drawn town vignettes, roads as dashed pen lines, fog as blank
parchment with a torn-edge boundary. If the direction lands, individual
elements get their own locked subjects (terrain tiles, town vignettes,
border style) and the SVG map adopts them piecemeal.

*v2 (Chris, playtest r2): each colour's region gets DISTINCT terrain and
hazards — not mountains everywhere.*

## Visual descriptor (locked)

```text
A top-down fantasy campaign map drawn in ink on aged parchment, as a
full-screen game map: five wedge-shaped regions arranged in a rough ring
around a central capital town, each washed in a faint heraldic tint and
each with its OWN terrain drawn as tiny hand-hatched pictorial symbols.
The cream-white region: open wheat fields, hedgerows, standing stones
and a lone cathedral spire. The sea-blue region: a ragged coastline with
estuaries, reed marshes, small islands and fishing weirs. The
dusk-violet region: black bog and mire, leafless dead trees, barrow
mounds with stone doors. The rust-red region: volcanic badlands, cracked
lava fields, cinder cones and one smoking caldera. The moss-green
region: deep old-growth forest with giant trees, root tangles and a
clearing of monoliths. Rivers as winding double pen lines, roads as
dashed brown lines linking small drawn town vignettes of clustered
rooftops. One corner of the map fades into blank untouched parchment
with a soft torn-edge boundary, as unexplored fog. A small brass compass
rose, thin double-rule map border, no text labels, no figures.
```
