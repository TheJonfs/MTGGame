## Part 2 — the defence (books 146–148)
- **`blockloss` (book 146) — ON TRIAL, not adopted**: a block that spends the blocker for no dearer attacker, while we are not dying (the opponent's board kills us within two turns), is refused (scores no better than −1); exempt a body with no power (book 97) and a death that pays (book 63). Against the Rabble (160 matches a pairing, paired, against off): the Writ −0.6, Grixis Clio −0.6, the Locks −1.2, the Aerie −1.2, the Watch −3.1 ± 3.0. Against a swarm of 1/1s a trade is a good block; the racing probe's extra blocks were the Emerald Snakes matchups' (bigger creatures, a different race). `trial: ["blockloss"]` keeps it. **Finding**: the brief's "the clock (our turns to kill against theirs)" made every low-power control deck "losing" every race, and nothing fired — `losingRace` reads only "the opponent kills us within N turns".
- **`sweepwait` (book 147) — adopted**: a board sweeper (each creature: Wrath, Retribution, Pyroclasm, the Wave, Savage Twister) waits for a board whose kills — their creatures' value less ours — reach **half** its mana value, unless the opponent's board kills us within three turns. At the brief's bar (the full mana value) it hurt against the swarm (the Jeskai Sweepers −4.1 against the red lists: four Goblin tokens are 2.0 of value, under a Wrath's 4); at half: **the Jeskai Sweepers +1.0 ± 0.7, the Locks +0.3, the Chancel +0.8, Mardu Control +2.6 ± 0.6** (+2.7 against the red lists). The sweeps probe: into a board of one or none 64% → 54%.
- **`payoffcost` (book 148) — adopted** (a correctness fix): a spell or ability that kills a creature of theirs while their death payoff is out (the Massacre, Blood Artist, the Usher), or sacrifices one of ours while theirs drains us, is refused when the drain is our last life. Chris's game 17 is the fixture (at one life the Grenade at Meliyan; a Grenade's Goblin into a Blood Artist). Measured: Massacrats −0.2 ± 0.1, the Pall −0.1, Mardu Control −0.2 — the position is rare.
- Ladder gate/mirror PASS (the three rules; then with `sweepwait` at half and `blockloss` on trial).
