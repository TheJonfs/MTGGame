# Concept: the dungeon interior map (S20 playtest round 1)

Direction-setting concept plate, not a shipping asset. The dungeon interior
currently reuses the overworld's parchment renderer; Chris asked (playtest
round 1, item 3) what an upgraded interior could look like. This explores a
darker register for the halls — carved stone corridors on charcoal ground,
the same ink-and-wash hand, so an interior reads as "under the mountain"
against the sunlit overworld. If the direction lands, the interior renderer
gets its own palette tokens (dark ground, chiseled wall hatching, torchlight
pools) and glyphs (chest, guardian door) as locked subjects.

## Visual descriptor (locked)

```text
A top-down dungeon map drawn in ink on dark slate-grey parchment, as a
full-screen game map: a branching network of carved stone corridors and
small chambers rendered as clean pale floor paths against a nearly black
hatched rock field, walls edged with fine chisel-mark hatching. Warm
pools of amber torchlight at junctions, a small drawn wooden chest
vignette in two side chambers, an ominous larger chamber at the far end
behind a drawn stone door flanked by braziers. The entrance at the map
edge marked by a descending stair symbol. Unexplored corridors fade into
darkness. Thin double-rule map border, no text labels, no figures.
```
